About

I start with the plant

I trained as a weaver and came to dye by accident, boiling onion skins in a borrowed pan in 2014. The colour held through forty washes. I have not bought a synthetic dye since.

The studio is a converted dairy on the edge of Lewes. Madder, weld and woad grow in the yard behind it. Walnut hulls come from a tree two fields over, with the farmer's blessing.

Every block is pearwood, carved by hand, and most take two days to cut. I print in small runs, wash each length three times and dry it on the line outside. A cloth from here has a slight wobble in the repeat. That is the hand, and I leave it in.

Blocks carved so far: 214. Cloth printed this year: 380 metres.

Services

What I make, and how long it takes

01Commissioned yardage
Printed and dyed cloth by the metre, for curtains, upholstery or garments. Runs start at ten metres.
Lead time 10 to 14 weeks
02Block carving
A pearwood block cut from your drawing or mine. You keep the block when the job is done.
Lead time 4 to 6 weeks
03Dye workshops
One-day sessions at the dairy for up to eight people. We dye with whatever is in season.
When Monthly, April to October
04Colour consulting
Plant colours for a product or interior, tested on your cloth, with swatches you can send to a mill.
Lead time 2 to 3 weeks
