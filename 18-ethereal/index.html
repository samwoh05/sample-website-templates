Light you feel before you see

I trained as a theatre electrician in Bergen and spent eleven years hanging lamps for other people. Now I design the whole arc: the first preset, the cue that makes a room go quiet, the last fade. I work with directors, curators and architects who want light to carry mood, not decoration. Most of my rigs use fewer lamps than you would expect, softened through gauze, haze and bounce.

Twelve years designing, after eleven as an electrician. Thirty-eight productions. Three permanent rooms.

Based in Bergen. Working across Scandinavia, and further by invitation.

How a project runs

  1. Listening

    I visit the room at the hour it will be used, and again at the hour it is hardest to light. I note where daylight falls, where people sit and what you want them to feel. You get a short written plot within a week.

    One to two days on site.

  2. Sketching

    I build the key moments in a 1:25 scale model with small lamps, gauze and haze, and film them so the team can react before any rig is hired. Changes here cost almost nothing.

    Two to four weeks.

  3. Focusing

    On site I rig, focus and write cues with your crew through technical rehearsals and previews. Most rooms end up with fewer lamps than the venue expected.

    One to three weeks on site.

  4. Handing over

    You get a cue book, focus photos and a training session, so the venue can run and restore the show without me. Permanent rooms get a seasonal check for the first year.

    Included in every project.
