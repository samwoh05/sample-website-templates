Hands first, screen second.

I trained as a silversmith before I studied industrial design. I still start every control with foam, a file and a pencil, not a wireframe.

My clients make things people touch every day: radios, hobs, thermostats, kiosks. I design the physical part and the screen together, then test both with people who use them at home, at work or in a care home. I have worked independently since 2016, in Swedish and English.

If a control needs a label to explain it, I keep working on it.

Four ways to work together.

Pick one, or combine them. Most projects start with control design and end with testing.

Control design

Knobs, buttons, sliders and switches. I set travel, detents and force, then hand you test parts to hold within the first two weeks.

What you get

  • Foam and printed test parts
  • Force and travel specs for your supplier
  • A tooling review with your factory
Process photo - 1200x900

Interface design

Screens, lights and sounds that sit next to a physical control. One system, so the dial and the display never disagree.

What you get

  • Screen flows and every state
  • LED and sound behaviour
  • Hand-off files for firmware
Process photo - 1200x900

Usability testing

Sessions with real users in homes, shops and care settings, including people with low vision or arthritis.

What you get

  • Test plan and recruitment
  • Recorded sessions with notes
  • A ranked list of fixes
Process photo - 1200x900

Design review

A two-day look at an existing product before it goes to tooling. You get a written review and a call with your team.

What you get

  • Hands-on review of samples
  • A written report
  • A follow-up call
